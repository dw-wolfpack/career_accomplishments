# Beyond Engineering

I have always been a runner. Apparently I told my parents at four years old that baseball was boring because there was not enough running. That part of me never really left. I have completed Ironman Lake Tahoe, Escape from Alcatraz, an ultramarathon, and more than 12 marathons, including an age-group win. Earlier, I competed in Nevada soccer's Olympic Development Program.

Before my software career, I worked through high school and college at a veterinary hospital. That early animal-care experience, years of outdoor training, and life with my dog Aoife later converged in NorthPaw: a product built around the readiness questions that activity trackers typically leave unanswered before and after an outing.

I have also gone shark-cage diving, skydived three times, surfed, and played a DJ set in a festival side room. None of that is a professional credential. It does say something about what I enjoy: preparing for something that scares me a little, then doing it anyway.

That experience is a big part of FitTrack. A training plan looks great on day one. The real test is what happens after a missed workout, a bad week, or a schedule change. I wanted the product to help with that part because long training blocks are never as clean as the original plan.

Engineering, writing, and endurance sports all get less glamorous after the first burst of excitement. I think I am at my best in the part that comes after that, when the only way forward is to keep learning and keep showing up.
